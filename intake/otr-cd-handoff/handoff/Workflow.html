How design gets made, reviewed, and shipped

Three places do work: an AI design surface, a source of truth repo, and Figma. Machines cover ground fast. Humans make it feel like OTR. The repo in the middle exists so those two never drift apart, and so nothing reaches engineering that a designer has not stamped.

The three surfaces

Surface 1

Design surface

Design, with AI • daily

  • Explores. Many directions in hours, not weeks.
  • Covers every state. 18 case states, not the 3 anyone wants to draw.
  • Writes decisions down. Every exploration closes with a verdict.
Strength: speed and coverage. Limit: it is wireframe grade. It is not the brand.

Surface 2

Source of truth

Claude Code • continuous

  • Holds canon. One answer to "which version is real."
  • Prints into Figma with brand variables attached, not loose colors.
  • Reads Figma back so human polish is never overwritten.
  • Reports drift the moment two surfaces disagree.
This is the new piece. Without it, the other two are two files nobody can reconcile.

Surface 3

Figma

Design and CEO • weekly

  • Polish. Type, rhythm, optical balance, real OTR craft.
  • Review. Comments land on frames, in the tool you already use.
  • Decide. Open calls get made live, on canvas, together.
  • Stamp. A human marks a frame ready for dev.
Strength: taste and authority. Limit: covering 18 states by hand is slow and nobody enjoys state 14.

The rule that prevents drift: nobody starts UI work, human or agent, without reading canon first. That preflight takes minutes and it is the entire reason two fast surfaces can run in parallel without producing two products.

How the Figma file is organised, so feedback lands cleanly

1
Components

The machine's workshop. Buttons, badges, cards, built once and reused.

2
Screens

Assembled screens, every state. This is what you look at most.

3
Proposed

Anything still open. Marked, so you know it is a question and not a claim.

4
Diffs

Colors and tokens with no brand answer yet. Design's to do list, not yours.

5
Ready for dev

Human only. Stamped, polished, final. Engineering reads this page and nothing else.

Agents write to pages 1 to 4. Only a designer moves a frame to page 5. That single rule is what keeps machine output from reaching engineering raw, and it is why speed upstream does not cost quality downstream.

How you give feedback

Step 1

Open the Screens page

Every state of every screen, side by side, freshly printed that week. Nothing to install, nothing to download.

Step 2

Comment on the frame

Pin a comment to the exact element. That is the whole protocol. Comments become decision records; nothing gets lost in a thread.

Step 3

Settle the open calls

The Proposed page holds what design could not decide alone. Fifteen minutes on canvas closes several at once.

Where the time actually comes from. Coverage is the expensive part of this product, not craft. A feed and a detail screen across 18 case states is days of drawing rectangles, and it is the work most likely to be skipped, which is how half finished states reach engineering. Machines do that breadth in minutes and never lose patience. The designer's hours then go where they are worth the most: making it look like OTR, and catching the things only a person catches. Review happens earlier because there is more to react to sooner, and rework is cheaper because a decision is recorded the moment it is made.
